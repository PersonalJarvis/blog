In September we used Personal Jarvis to build Personal Jarvis, all day, most days. If you price every token of that month the way the AI providers price their APIs, the work was worth $15,224.

Our API keys were billed $216.65. And $189.45 of that came from one coding agent that runs on our own key, most of it in a single evening.

The other $15,007 ran on flat monthly plans, and Jarvis still prices every token of it. This post covers where you see those numbers in the app, how a token becomes a price, what all those tokens actually were, and what broke while we taught Jarvis to count. In this post, "we" means the people building Personal Jarvis and the coding agents that do much of the typing.

## Where you see it

Everything below comes from one place in the app: **Settings → Activity → Spend**, a section called **Spend & Tokens**. The same section runs live on the "Spend" monitor in Mission Control, the room for the whole agent fleet in the Jarvis Verse office.

The controls are the whole story in small. The **range** picks 7, 30 or 90 days or everything. The **area switch** narrows the numbers to the assistant, its voice, or the Agentic IDE. The **billing switch** is the important one: **Billed to keys** shows money that actually left an API key, **On subscriptions** shows work done on a flat plan, and **All** shows both. Dollars can be shown in euros at a fixed exchange rate.

## Two numbers for every day

Every call Jarvis records gets two answers: what it would cost through the provider's API, and whether that money was actually billed. Work on a subscription, such as a Claude Code or Codex login, is not shown as zero. It is priced at what the same tokens would cost through the API and labelled as covered by a plan. That is what lets you see what a plan is worth to you.

## How a token becomes a price

Jarvis prices a call with three counts and a rate card. **Input** is new text the model reads. **Cache reads** are text the provider kept from the call before and lets the model read again at a discount: a tenth of the input rate for most models, a quarter for Gemini and Grok. **Output** is what the model writes. Where the price comes from depends on what the tool tells us. When a tool reports its own price for a call, as Grok Build does, Jarvis keeps that number. Otherwise it applies its rate card. A local model, through Ollama for example, costs nothing. A model Jarvis has no rate for is counted as **unpriced**, never as free: in September that was 7.8 million tokens across 50 calls, and the Spend section shows them on their own tile so a gap cannot hide inside a small total.

## What a subscription number does not tell you

The $15,007 covered by plans is not money we spent. It is what the same tokens would have cost through the API. Jarvis cannot know what your plan costs or how close you are to its limits in dollars, so it does not pretend to: it shows the list price of the work and leaves the comparison to you. If a plan costs you a fixed amount per month and Jarvis shows you ten times that in covered work, the plan is paying for itself.

## What broke on the way

Counting tokens sounds like adding numbers. It took several rounds to get right, and each one changed the total by a lot.

**A $37,000 bill that never existed.** On August 24 the Spend section showed one machine a month of roughly $37,000. Two Codex habits caused it. Codex counts cached tokens inside its input number, where Claude Code reports them separately, so the cache was priced twice. And a forked Codex thread replays its whole history into a new file, so every fork looked like hours of fresh work. Jarvis now subtracts the cached share and recognises a replayed call by Codex's own running total.

**Twice the tokens from the same answer.** Claude Code writes one model response as several lines, one per content block, and each line repeats the same usage numbers. On a sample transcript that was 64 lines for 31 responses. Jarvis now counts a response by the id the API gave it, not by the line.

**A fifth of all Claude Code calls were invisible.** When Claude Code starts a subagent, that subagent writes its own transcript in a subfolder. Until August 27 nothing read those files. On the machine we measured, they held 14,466 of 68,937 responses.

**The past shrank every night.** Claude Code deletes old sessions after 25 days on that machine, and when a transcript disappeared, its cost disappeared with it. The lifetime total got a little smaller every night. Jarvis now keeps what it has read as a ledger, so deleting a transcript no longer deletes the money it cost.

**A rebuild showed $8,000 of a $13,600 month.** An early version threw its index away and re-read everything when a counting rule changed, and showed the half-built number in the meantime. Now it corrects rows in place, and while it is still reading it says so. That is the dashed notice in figure 1.

**A voice key paid for the wiki.** On September 29, an OpenAI key added for voice paid about $0.30 of background wiki calls after a two-minute chat. Small, but nobody had asked for it. Since then, background work only runs on subscriptions or local models while a subscription is connected, and waits rather than falling back to a key.

**Make a gap visible, never zero.** Every one of these bugs made a number quietly wrong rather than loudly missing. The section now shows unpriced tokens on their own tile and says when it is still counting, because a confident wrong total is worse than an honest incomplete one.

## If you run Jarvis yourself

- **Open Settings → Activity → Spend.** Leave it on 30 days, switch to **Billed to keys** once to see what you actually pay, then to **On subscriptions** to see what your plans cover.
- **Check the Unpriced tile.** If it is not zero, some model has no rate yet, and its work is missing from every dollar figure.
- **Open your most expensive day** in the Daily report. It usually names one tool and one evening.
- **Look for keys in the coding agents.** A coding agent on a pay-per-token key costs far more than voice and background work together.
- **A chat shows its own receipt.** Under each agent turn that reported a cost, the chat shows its tokens and price.

If a number in your Spend section looks wrong, open an issue on [GitHub](https://github.com/PersonalJarvis/PersonalJarvis/issues) with the day it happened.
